Explanation of the transformation:

The background environment is rendered using a full-screen quad defined
directly in clip space. Because the quad already covers the entire
viewport, no projection or view transformation is applied when rendering
the background geometry.

To obtain correct sampling directions for the cube map, the clip-space
positions of the background quad are transformed into world-space
directions. This is done using the matrix Mtex, which is constructed from
the inverse view and inverse projection matrices, with the translation
component removed. As a result, Mtex converts clip-space coordinates into
direction vectors originating from the camera.

In the fragment shader, the clip-space position is multiplied by Mtex and
divided by its homogeneous component to obtain a world-space direction.
This direction is normalized and used as the lookup direction for the
cube map texture.

For the sphere, cube map sampling is based on the world-space surface
normal. The surface normal is transformed by the model matrix and
normalized before being used as a texture lookup direction. This causes
the environment to appear mapped onto the sphere surface.

Both the background and the sphere use the same cube map texture, but
different direction computations. The background represents the
environment at infinity, while the sphere reflects the environment based
on its surface orientation. As the camera orbits the scene, both the
background and the sphere update consistently, creating a coherent
environment-mapped result.
