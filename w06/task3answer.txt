Earth texture mapping using surface normals:

The Earth texture is mapped onto the sphere by computing texture
coordinates directly in the fragment shader. The surface normal of
each fragment is used as an intermediate representation, since the
normals of a unit sphere correspond to points on the sphere surface.

The normal vector is first normalized and interpreted as a point on the
unit sphere. Spherical coordinates are then derived from this vector.
The horizontal texture coordinate u is computed using atan2 on the x
and z components of the normal, ensuring correct handling of all
quadrants. The vertical texture coordinate v is computed using acos of
the y component of the normal. The resulting u and v values are scaled
to the range [0, 1] and used for texture lookup.

The color sampled from the Earth texture is used as the diffuse
reflection coefficient. The sphere is illuminated using a directional
light and a constant ambient term, producing a diffuse shaded globe.

Filtering choice:

The Earth texture has high resolution, which causes minification
artifacts when the sphere rotates, especially in detailed regions such
as mountain ranges. To reduce shimmering and aliasing, mipmapping is
enabled and linear mipmap filtering is used. This approach selects
lower-resolution texture levels when the surface is far from the camera
and blends smoothly between mipmap levels.

Linear filtering provides a good balance between reducing aliasing and
preserving texture detail, avoiding excessive blurring while producing
stable visual results during camera orbiting.
