Texture filtering and mipmapping:

Texture filtering determines how texture values are sampled when texture
coordinates do not map exactly to a single texel.

Magnification:
Magnification occurs when a texture is displayed larger than its resolution.
Using nearest filtering selects the closest texel, which results in a blocky
and pixelated appearance. Using linear filtering interpolates between nearby
texels, producing a smoother result but slightly blurred edges.

Minification:
Minification occurs when a texture is displayed smaller than its resolution.
Without mipmapping, nearest filtering causes strong aliasing and flickering,
while linear filtering reduces some artifacts but still produces shimmering
patterns.

Mipmapping:
Mipmapping reduces minification artifacts by selecting a prefiltered,
lower-resolution version of the texture. This significantly reduces aliasing
when the textured surface is far from the camera.

Mipmap filtering:
Nearest mipmap filtering selects the closest mipmap level and can produce
visible transitions between levels. Linear mipmap filtering blends between
mipmap levels, resulting in smoother transitions and more stable visuals.

Wrapping modes:
Repeat wrapping causes the texture to tile across the surface, while
clamp-to-edge stretches the border texels when texture coordinates exceed the
range. Wrapping affects how textures behave outside the standard coordinate
range but does not change filtering quality.

Overall:
Nearest filtering preserves sharp texel boundaries but introduces aliasing.
Linear filtering improves visual smoothness. Mipmapping is essential for
reducing minification artifacts and producing stable textures at varying
distances.
