Projection shadows

Advantages:
- Simple to implement and easy to understand.
- Computationally cheap since it only requires projecting geometry.
- No additional textures or depth comparisons are needed.

Disadvantages:
- No self-shadowing; objects cannot cast shadows on themselves.
- Works reliably only on simple surfaces such as a flat ground plane.
- Produces unrealistic artifacts such as stretched or intersecting shadows.
- Shadows can extend outside the intended surface unless explicitly clipped.
- Not suitable for complex scenes with multiple objects and receivers.

Shadow mapping

Advantages:
- Supports self-shadowing and shadows between arbitrary objects.
- Works for complex geometry and multiple shadow-receiving surfaces.
- More physically plausible, since shadows are based on visibility from the light source.
- Can be extended with filtering techniques (e.g. PCF) to produce softer shadows.

Disadvantages:
- More complex to implement, requiring additional coordinate spaces and render passes.
- Higher memory and performance cost due to shadow map textures.
- Susceptible to artifacts such as shadow acne and Peter-panning, which require bias tuning.
- Shadow quality depends on the resolution of the shadow map and light frustum setup.
